## 1. Overview

Welcome to your first unit of Serbian! This unit focuses on the absolute essentials you need for your first interactions. We will learn basic greetings, politeness phrases, and simple communication phrases that will help you navigate your first days in a Serbian-speaking region.

#### A Note from the Founder (Jacksenn)

> Let's be honest: at the beginning, learning a new language can be incredibly frustrating. It's hard to remember vocabulary, and you often feel like you'll never understand how to speak it. You might feel insecure in a restaurant or hesitate to use what you've learned. But here is our promise: Do not put pressure on yourself. You must have the courage to simply try. When people see that you are making an effort to speak their language, you will always bring a smile to their face. We are here to give you that courage.

#### Learning Objectives

By the end of this unit, **we** will be able to:
1. Greet people formally and informally.
2. Use basic politeness phrases like "please," "thank you," and "excuse me."
3. Say "yes" and "no."
4. Ask if someone speaks English.
5. Say that we do not understand.
6. Introduce ourselves using "Ja sam..." (I am...) and "Zovem se..." (My name is...).
7. Use the present tense of the verb "biti" (to be) for "I," "you," and "he/she."

## 3. Grammar (Gramatika)

In Serbian, grammar is key to forming correct sentences. Let's start with the absolute basics.

### A. The Verb "biti" (to be)

The verb `biti` (to be) is irregular and essential. We will learn the present tense forms for "I," "you" (informal), and "he/she." In Serbian, you often omit the pronoun (`ja`, `ti`, `on`, `ona`) because the verb form already tells you who is doing the action.

**Affirmative (Positive) Forms**

| Pronoun | Verb | Serbian Example | English Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ja (I) | **sam** | Ja **sam** turista. | I am a tourist. |
| Ti (You) | **si** | Ti **si** student. | You are a student. |
| On/Ona (He/She) | **je** | On **je** konobar. | He is a waiter. |

**Negative Forms**

To make a negative sentence, you use the negative forms. These are single words formed by joining `ne` (no) with the verb.

| Pronoun | Verb | Serbian Example | English Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ja (I) | **nisam** | Ja **nisam** student. | I am not a student. |
| Ti (You) | **nisi** | Ti **nisi** ovde. | You are not here. |
| On/Ona (He/She) | **nije** | Ona **nije** iz Srbije. | She is not from Serbia. |

### B. Introduction to Grammatical Gender

Every noun in Serbian has a grammatical gender: masculine, feminine, or neuter. You can often guess the gender from the noun's ending in its basic (nominative) form.

- **Masculine** nouns often end in a consonant.
  - Examples: **hotel**, **pasoš**, **ključ**
- **Feminine** nouns usually end in `-a`.
  - Examples: **Srbija**, **Engleska**
- **Neuter** nouns often end in `-o` or `-e`.
  - Example: **ime** (name)

Gender is important because it affects adjectives and other words related to the noun. We will explore this more in later units.

### C. The Genitive Case with "iz" (from)

In English, we use prepositions like "from," "of," or "with" before a noun. In Serbian, these prepositions often cause the noun to change its ending. This change is called a "case."

The preposition **iz** means "from" and requires the **Genitive case**. For now, we will only learn how feminine nouns ending in `-a` change.

Rule: Feminine nouns ending in `-a` change their ending to `-e` after `iz`.

- **Srbija** (Serbia) becomes **iz Srbije** (from Serbia).
- **Engleska** (England) becomes **iz Engleske** (from England).

**Examples:**
1. Ja sam **iz Srbije**. (I am from Serbia.)
2. Ona je **iz Engleske**. (She is from England.)
3. Pitanje: **Odakle** si ti? (Where are you from?) Odgovor: Ja sam **iz Srbije**. (I am from Serbia.)

## 6. Cultural Note: The Importance of "Vi"

In Serbian, like in many other European languages, there are two ways to say "you": **ti** (informal) and **Vi** (formal).

- **Ti** is used with friends, family, children, and people your own age in informal settings.
- **Vi** is used to show respect to older people, strangers, and in any formal or professional situation (like in a shop, hotel, or office). It is always written with a capital "V".

When in doubt, always use **Vi**. It is a sign of politeness and respect. If the other person wants to switch to the informal "ti," they will usually suggest it. Using "ti" with a stranger who is older than you can be considered rude.
