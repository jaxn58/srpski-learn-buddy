# Module 1: Ankommen (Arrival)
## Unit 1: First Words

**Base Language:** English

## 1. Overview

#### A Note from the Founder (Jacksenn)

> "The very first words you speak in a new language are the most important. They break the ice, they show respect, and they open doors. When I first arrived in Montenegro, I was terrified to speak. But the moment I said 'Dobar dan' to a shopkeeper and she smiled back, I knew I was on the right path. This unit is about giving you those first essential words—the ones that will make you feel human, not helpless. Don't worry about perfection. Just say hello, say thank you, and watch the magic happen."

#### Learning Objectives

By the end of this unit, **we** will be able to:

1. **Greet people** in formal and informal situations using appropriate Serbian phrases.
2. **Say goodbye** politely.
3. **Be polite** by using "thank you," "please," and "excuse me."
4. **Overcome the language barrier** by saying "I don't speak Serbian" and "Do you speak English?"
5. **Say yes and no** confidently.
6. **Introduce ourselves** with our name (very basic).

## 3. Grammar (Gramatika)

### A. Verb "biti" (to be) - The Essential Forms

We will learn the **three most important forms** of the verb **"biti" (to be)** in this unit. The full conjugation comes in Unit 2.

#### Conjugation Table (Partial)

| Person | Serbian | English |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ja** | sam | I am |
| **Ti** | si | You are (informal) |
| **On/Ona/Ono** | je | He/She/It is |

**Examples:**
- Ja sam Alex. (I am Alex.)
- Ja sam turista. (I am a tourist.)
- Ti si iz Engleske? (Are you from England?)
- On je konobar. (He is a waiter.)
- Ona je turistkinja. (She is a tourist.)

**Note:** In casual speech, "Ja" and "Ti" are often dropped: "Sam Alex." "Si turista?" But for beginners, it's safer to include the pronoun.

---

### B. "Govorim..." (I speak...) - Overcoming the Language Barrier

We will learn only the **1st person singular** of the verb **"govoriti" (to speak)**.

**Form:** Govorim

**Examples:**
- Govorim engleski. (I speak English.)
- Ne govorim srpski. (I don't speak Serbian.)

**Negative Form:** Just add **"ne"** before the verb.

### B. Dialogue 1: At the Border (Na granici)

**Scene:** Alex arrives at the Serbian border and goes through passport control.

| Role | Serbian | English |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Border Officer** | Dobar dan. Pasoš, molim. | Good day. Passport, please. |
| **Alex** | Dobar dan. Izvolite. | Good day. Here you are. |
| **Border Officer** | Hvala. Turista? | Thank you. Tourist? |
| **Alex** | Da, turista. | Yes, tourist. |
| **Border Officer** | Dobro. Doviđenja. | Good. Goodbye. |
| **Alex** | Doviđenja. | Goodbye. |

### C. Dialogue 2: In the Taxi (U taksiju)

**Scene:** Alex gets into a taxi and tries to communicate with the driver.

| Role | Serbian | English |

| **Taxi Driver** | Molim. | You're welcome. |

### E. Dialogue 4: Meeting Someone (Upoznavanje)

**Scene:** Alex meets a local person at a café and tries to introduce himself.

| Role | Serbian | English |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Local** | Zdravo! | Hello! |
| **Alex** | Zdravo! Ja sam Alex. | Hello! I am Alex. |
| **Local** | Drago mi je. Ja sam Marko. Ti si turista? | Nice to meet you. I am Marko. Are you a tourist? |
| **Alex** | Da, ja sam turista. | Yes, I am a tourist. |
| **Local** | Dobrodošao! Govorite li srpski? | Welcome! Do you speak Serbian? |

## C. Cultural Note: The Importance of Greetings in Serbian Culture

- **Greetings are sacred:** In Serbia and Montenegro, greeting people is not optional—it's a sign of respect. Always say "Dobar dan" when entering a shop, restaurant, or office.
- **"Molim" is magic:** This word means both "please" and "you're welcome." It's incredibly versatile and shows politeness.
- **Don't skip "Doviđenja":** Always say goodbye when leaving. It's considered rude to just walk out without a farewell.
- **Smiles go a long way:** Even if your pronunciation is terrible, a smile and an attempt to speak Serbian will be warmly received.
