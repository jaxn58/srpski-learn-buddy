# Module 1: Ankommen (Arrival)
## Unit 2: Who Are You?

**Description:** Learn to introduce yourself, ask about names and origins, and discuss nationalities and professions.

**Base Language:** English
**Target Language:** Serbian

---

## 1. Overview

This unit builds on Unit 1 by teaching you how to introduce yourself more completely. You will learn to say your full name, ask others about their names and origins, and discuss nationalities and professions. You will also learn the complete conjugation of two essential verbs: "biti" (to be) and "zvati se" (to be called).

#### A Note from the Founder (Jacksenn)

> "One of the most beautiful experiences I've had in Montenegro is seeing people's faces light up when I try to share something about myself in Serbian—where I'm from, how old I am, where I'm staying, or how long I've been here. Montenegrins genuinely appreciate when you make the effort to tell them a bit about yourself, even if your Serbian is far from perfect. This unit gives you the tools to do exactly that. Don't worry about making mistakes—what matters is that you're trying to connect."

#### Learning Objectives

By the end of this unit, you will be able to:

1. Introduce yourself with your full name
2. Ask someone their name and where they're from
3. Use the complete conjugation of the verb "biti" (to be)
4. Use the complete conjugation of the verb "zvati se" (to be called)
5. Name countries and nationalities in Serbian
6. Discuss basic professions
7. Ask and answer about age
8. Use numbers 1-30

#### Progression

**Prerequisites (Known from Unit 1):**
- Basic greetings: Zdravo, Dobar dan, Dobro veče, Laku noć, Doviđenja

- First person of "govoriti": Govorim, Ne govorim

## 3. Grammar (Gramatika)

### A. Verb "biti" (to be) - Complete Conjugation

In Unit 1, you learned three basic forms of "biti" (sam, si, je). Now we complete the conjugation with all six persons.

#### Affirmative Forms

| Person | Serbian | English | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ja | sam | I am | Ja sam student. (I am a student.) |
| Ti | si | You are (informal) | Ti si iz Srbije. (You are from Serbia.) |
| On/Ona | je | He/She is | On je lekar. (He is a doctor.) |
| Mi | smo | We are | Mi smo iz Nemačke. (We are from Germany.) |
| Vi | ste | You are (formal/plural) | Vi ste učitelj. (You are a teacher.) |
| Oni/One | su | They are | Oni su studenti. (They are students.) |

#### Negative Forms

| Person | Serbian | English | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ja | nisam | I am not | Ja nisam lekar. (I am not a doctor.) |
| Ti | nisi | You are not | Ti nisi iz Engleske. (You are not from England.) |
| On/Ona | nije | He/She is not | Ona nije studentkinja. (She is not a student.) |
| Mi | nismo | We are not | Mi nismo iz Amerike. (We are not from America.) |
| Vi | niste | You are not | Vi niste turista. (You are not a tourist.) |
| Oni/One | nisu | They are not | Oni nisu studenti. (They are not students.) |

**Note:** "Vi" is used both for formal address (to one person) and for plural (to multiple people).

---

### B. Verb "zvati se" (to be called) - Complete Conjugation

The verb "zvati se" is a **reflexive verb** (it includes "se"). It means "to be called" or "to call oneself."

#### Conjugation

| Person | Serbian | English | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ja | zovem se | My name is | Ja se zovem Ana. (My name is Ana.) |
| Ti | zoveš se | Your name is | Ti se zoveš Marko. (Your name is Marko.) |
| On/Ona | zove se | His/Her name is | On se zove Petar. (His name is Petar.) |
| Mi | zovemo se | Our names are | Mi se zovemo Ana i Marko. |
| Vi | zovete se | Your name(s) is/are | Vi se zovete...? (What is your name?) |
| Oni/One | zovu se | Their names are | Oni se zovu Petar i Ana. |

**Question Form:**
- **Kako se zoveš?** (What's your name? - informal)
- **Kako se zovete?** (What's your name? - formal/plural)

---

### C. Formal vs. Informal "You" (Ti vs. Vi)

Serbian has two ways to say "you":

| Form | When to Use | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ti** (informal) | Friends, family, children, peers | Kako se zoveš? |
| **Vi** (formal) | Strangers, older people, professional settings | Kako se zovete? |

**Important:** Always use "Vi" when meeting someone for the first time or in formal situations.

### B. Dialogue 1: First Meeting at a Café (Prvo upoznavanje u kafeu)

**Scene:** Alex meets Ana at a café in Belgrade.

| Role | Serbian | English |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ana** | Zdravo! Kako se zoveš? | Hello! What's your name? |
| **Alex** | Zdravo! Zovem se Alex. A ti? | Hello! My name is Alex. And you? |
| **Ana** | Ja sam Ana. Drago mi je. | I am Ana. Nice to meet you. |
| **Alex** | Drago mi je. Odakle si? | Nice to meet you. Where are you from? |
| **Ana** | Ja sam iz Srbije, iz Beograda. A ti? | I am from Serbia, from Belgrade. And you? |
| **Alex** | Ja sam iz Engleske. | I am from England. |
| **Ana** | Odlično! Šta radiš? | Excellent! What do you do? |
| **Alex** | Ja sam student. A ti? | I am a student. And you? |
| **Ana** | Ja sam učiteljica. | I am a teacher. |

## Cultural Note: Formality and Full Names in Serbian Culture

In Serbian culture, **formality** is important when meeting someone for the first time or in professional settings. Always use **"Vi"** (formal "you") until the other person suggests switching to **"Ti"** (informal "you").

**Full Names:** In formal situations, Serbians often introduce themselves with their **full name** (first name + last name). For example: "Zovem se Petar Petrović."

**Patronymic Last Names:** Many Serbian last names end in **-ović** or **-ević**, which originally meant "son of." For example, "Petrović" means "son of Petar."

**Hospitality:** Serbians are known for their hospitality. When you meet someone, they will often say **"Dobrodošli!"** (Welcome!) and may invite you for coffee or rakija (traditional brandy).

**Montenegrin Variants:** In Montenegro, some words are pronounced differently:
- **Lekar** (doctor) → **Ljekar**
- **Lekarka** (female doctor) → **Ljekarka**
