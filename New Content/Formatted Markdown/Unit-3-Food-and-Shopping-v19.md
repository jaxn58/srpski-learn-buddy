# Module 1: Ankommen (Arrival)
## Unit 3: Food & Shopping

**Description:** Learn to order food and drinks at a konoba/café and shop for groceries at supermarkets and the green market.

**Base Language:** English
**Target Language:** Serbian

## 1. Overview

Welcome to Unit 3! In this unit, you will learn how to navigate two essential daily activities in Serbian-speaking regions: eating out and shopping for food. You'll learn to order at a konoba (traditional restaurant) or café, and to shop at a supermarket or the green market (zelena pijaca), where locals buy fresh fruits, vegetables, and other products.

#### A Note from the Founder (Jacksenn)

> "Coffee culture is huge in Montenegro and Serbia—people spend hours at cafés, and it's a central part of social life. Equally important is the green market (pijaca), where you'll find the freshest produce and have real conversations with local vendors. These two places—the café and the market—are where you'll truly feel connected to daily life here. Don't be shy about ordering or asking for what you need. Even if you make mistakes, people will appreciate your effort and often help you along the way."

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this unit, you will be able to:
- Order food and drinks at a konoba, restaurant, or café
- Ask for the menu and the bill
- Express what you want using "hteti" and "želeti"
- Shop at a supermarket or green market
- Ask if they have something ("Imate li...?")
- Buy fruits, vegetables, and meat
- Ask for prices and quantities
- Use numbers with confidence in shopping contexts
- Understand basic gender agreement with numbers

## 3. Grammar (Gramatika)

### A. Verb "hteti" (to want) - Essential Forms

The verb "hteti" is used to express what you want. It's one of the most important verbs for ordering and shopping.

**Full Conjugation:**

| Person | Affirmative | Negative |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ja | hoću | neću |
| Ti | hoćeš | nećeš |
| On/Ona/Ono | hoće | neće |
| Mi | hoćemo | nećemo |
| Vi | hoćete | nećete |
| Oni/One/Ona | hoće | neće |

**Short Forms (Clitic):**

In spoken Serbian, short forms are very common:

| Person | Short Form |
| :--- | :--- |
| Ja | ću |
| Ti | ćeš |
| On/Ona/Ono | će |
| Mi | ćemo |
| Vi | ćete |
| Oni/One/Ona | će |

**Examples:**
- Ja hoću kafu. (I want coffee.)
- Hoću kafu. (I want coffee.) - "Ja" can be omitted
- Hoćeš li vodu? (Do you want water?)
- Ne hoću ništa. (I don't want anything.)
- Hoćemo da jedemo. (We want to eat.)

**Note:** "Hteti" is also used to form the future tense, but in this unit, we focus on its meaning "to want."

---

### B. Verb "želeti" (to wish/want) - Polite Form

The verb "želeti" is a more polite way to express wanting something. It's often used in formal situations or when being extra polite.

**Full Conjugation:**

| Person | Affirmative | Negative |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ja | želim | ne želim |
| Ti | želiš | ne želiš |
| On/Ona/Ono | želi | ne želi |
| Mi | želimo | ne želimo |
| Vi | želite | ne želite |
| Oni/One/Ona | žele | ne žele |

**Examples:**
- Želim kafu, molim. (I would like coffee, please.)
- Šta želite? (What would you like?)
- Želim da kupim hleb. (I want to buy bread.)
- Ne želim ništa. (I don't want anything.)

**Difference between "hteti" and "želeti":**
- **Hteti** = direct, straightforward "to want"
- **Želeti** = polite, "to wish" or "would like"

---

### C. Verb "imati" (to have) - Essential for Shopping

The verb "imati" is crucial for asking if something is available ("Do you have...?") and for saying what you have.

**Full Conjugation:**

| Person | Affirmative | Negative |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ja | imam | nemam |
| Ti | imaš | nemaš |
| On/Ona/Ono | ima | nema |
| Mi | imamo | nemamo |
| Vi | imate | nemate |
| Oni/One/Ona | imaju | nemaju |

**Important Usage:**

The third person singular "ima/nema" is used to say "there is/there is no...":
- Ima hleba. (There is bread.)
- Nema jabuka. (There are no apples.)

**Examples:**
- Imam novac. (I have money.)
- Imaš li vreme? (Do you have time?)
- Imate li jabuke? (Do you have apples?) - asking a vendor
- Ima svežeg voća. (There is fresh fruit.)
- Nemam kesa. (I don't have bags.)

**Question Form:**
- Imate li...? (Do you have...?) - formal/polite
- Imaš li...? (Do you have...?) - informal

### D. Numbers with Gender Agreement (Review from Unit 2)

You learned numbers 1-30 in Unit 2. In this unit, we review how numbers 1 and 2 change based on the gender of the noun, which is essential for ordering and shopping.

**Number 1 (jedan/jedna/jedno):**

| Gender | Form | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Masculine | jedan | jedan hleb (one bread) |
| Feminine | jedna | jedna jabuka (one apple) |
| Neuter | jedno | jedno jaje (one egg) |

**Number 2 (dva/dve):**

| Gender | Form | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Masculine/Neuter | dva | dva hleba (two breads), dva jajeta (two eggs) |
| Feminine | dve | dve jabuke (two apples) |

**Numbers 3-10:**

Numbers 3-10 do not change based on gender:
- tri, četiri, pet, šest, sedam, osam, devet, deset

**Examples:**
- Hoću jedan sendvič. (I want one sandwich.) - masculine
- Hoću jednu kafu. (I want one coffee.) - feminine
- Hoću dva piva. (I want two beers.) - masculine
- Hoću dve kafe. (I want two coffees.) - feminine
- Hoću tri jabuke. (I want three apples.)

### E. Basic Accusative Case (for Ordering/Buying)

When you order or buy something, the object (what you're ordering) goes into the **Accusative case**. For now, you don't need to memorize all the rules—just be aware that some nouns change slightly.

**Common patterns:**

**Masculine nouns (ending in consonant):**
- Usually no change, or add -a for animate objects
- hleb → hleb (bread stays the same)
- sendvič → sendvič (sandwich stays the same)

**Feminine nouns (ending in -a):**
- Change -a to -u
- kafa → kafu (coffee)
- voda → vodu (water)
- jabuka → jabuku (apple)

**Neuter nouns (ending in -o or -e):**
- Usually no change
- vino → vino (wine)
- pivo → pivo (beer)

**Examples:**
- Hoću kafu. (I want coffee.) - kafa → kafu
- Hoću vodu. (I want water.) - voda → vodu
- Hoću hleb. (I want bread.) - hleb → hleb (no change)
- Hoću pivo. (I want beer.) - pivo → pivo (no change)

**Don't worry too much about this yet!** In this unit, focus on understanding the basic pattern. You'll learn more about cases in later units.

### Dialogue 1: Ordering at a Café

**Setting:** Ana and Marko are at a café in Budva.

**Ana:** Dobar dan! Meni, molim.  
*(Good day! Menu, please.)*

**Konobar:** Izvolite.  
*(Here you are.)*

**Ana:** Hoću jednu kafu, molim.  
*(I want one coffee, please.)*

**Konobar:** Tursku ili espreso?  
*(Turkish or espresso?)*

**Ana:** Tursku, molim.  
*(Turkish, please.)*

**Marko:** A ja hoću čaj. Imate li voćni čaj?  
*(And I want tea. Do you have fruit tea?)*

**Konobar:** Da, imamo.  
*(Yes, we have.)*

**Marko:** Dobro. Hoću voćni čaj, molim.  
*(Good. I want fruit tea, please.)*

**Konobar:** Hoćete li nešto za jesti?  
*(Would you like something to eat?)*

**Ana:** Da, hoću jedan burek.  
*(Yes, I want one burek.)*

**Marko:** A ja hoću sendvič sa sirom.  
*(And I want a sandwich with cheese.)*

**Konobar:** U redu. Hvala.  
*(Alright. Thank you.)*

---

### Dialogue 2: At the Green Market (Zelena pijaca)

**Setting:** Lena is shopping for fruits and vegetables at the green market.

**Lena:** Dobar dan! Imate li svežih jabuka?  
*(Good day! Do you have fresh apples?)*

**Prodavačica:** Da, imamo. Domaće su.  
*(Yes, we have. They're local.)*

**Lena:** Koliko košta kilo?  
*(How much does a kilo cost?)*

**Prodavačica:** 120 dinara.  
*(120 dinars.)*

**Lena:** Dobro. Hoću dva kilo, molim.  
*(Good. I want two kilos, please.)*

**Prodavačica:** Izvolite. Još nešto?  
*(Here you are. Anything else?)*

**Lena:** Da, hoću i paradajz. Koliko košta?  
*(Yes, I also want tomatoes. How much do they cost?)*

**Prodavačica:** 150 dinara kilo.  
*(150 dinars per kilo.)*

**Lena:** Dajte mi kilo, molim.  
*(Give me a kilo, please.)*

**Prodavačica:** Izvolite. To je 390 dinara.  
*(Here you are. That's 390 dinars.)*

**Lena:** Hvala. Izvolite.  
*(Thank you. Here you are.)*

**Prodavačica:** Hvala. Prijatno!  
*(Thank you. Enjoy!)*

---

### Dialogue 3: At the Supermarket

**Setting:** Petar is shopping at a supermarket.

**Petar:** Izvinite, gde je hleb?  
*(Excuse me, where is the bread?)*

**Radnik:** Tamo, levo.  
*(There, on the left.)*

**Petar:** Hvala.  
*(Thank you.)*

*(At the checkout)*

**Kasirka:** Dobar dan. Imate li kesu?  
*(Good day. Do you have a bag?)*

**Petar:** Ne, nemam. Mogu li jednu kesu?  
*(No, I don't have. Can I have one bag?)*

**Kasirka:** Da, naravno. To je 850 dinara.  
*(Yes, of course. That's 850 dinars.)*

**Petar:** Izvolite.  
*(Here you are.)*

**Kasirka:** Hvala. Doviđenja.  
*(Thank you. Goodbye.)*

---

### Dialogue 4: Asking for the Bill

**Setting:** Stefan and Jelena are at a konoba.

**Stefan:** Račun, molim.  
*(Bill, please.)*

**Konobar:** Izvolite. To je 1450 dinara.  
*(Here you are. That's 1450 dinars.)*

**Stefan:** Hvala. Izvolite.  
*(Thank you. Here you are.)*

**Konobar:** Hvala vam. Prijatno!  
*(Thank you. Enjoy!)*

**Jelena:** Hvala, doviđenja!  
*(Thank you, goodbye!)*

## 6. Cultural Note

### Food Culture in Montenegro and Serbia

**Café Culture:**
Coffee is a central part of social life in Montenegro and Serbia. People spend hours at cafés, chatting with friends and family. Turkish coffee (turska kafa) is traditional and strong, served in small cups. Espresso and cappuccino are also very popular. Don't be surprised if your coffee comes with a glass of water!

**The Green Market (Zelena pijaca):**
The green market is where locals buy fresh fruits, vegetables, and other products. It's a vibrant, social place where you can practice your Serbian and get to know vendors. Prices are usually per kilo, and you can often negotiate or ask for a taste before buying. The produce is seasonal and fresh, and vendors take pride in their products.

**Konoba:**
A konoba is a traditional restaurant, especially common in coastal areas like Montenegro. It's usually family-run and serves local dishes. The atmosphere is warm and welcoming, and the food is often homemade. Don't rush—meals are meant to be enjoyed slowly!

**Tipping:**
Tipping is appreciated but not mandatory. In restaurants, rounding up the bill or leaving 10% is considered generous. In cafés, small change is fine.

**"Prijatno!"**
This is the Serbian equivalent of "Bon appétit!" You'll hear it from waiters and vendors. It literally means "pleasant" and is a way of wishing you an enjoyable meal.

**Shopping Bags:**
In supermarkets, you usually have to pay for plastic bags (kesa). Many people bring their own reusable bags. At the green market, vendors often provide bags for free, but it's polite to bring your own.

**Bargaining:**
At the green market, light bargaining is sometimes acceptable, especially if you're buying in larger quantities. However, be respectful—vendors work hard, and prices are usually fair.

**Montenegrin Variants:**
- **Hleb** (bread) → **Hljeb** in Montenegro
- **Lekovi** (medicines) → **Lijekovi** in Montenegro

These are minor pronunciation differences, and both are understood everywhere.
