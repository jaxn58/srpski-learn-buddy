## Unit 3: Food & Shopping (Hrana i Kupovina)

**Description:** Learn to order food at cafés and restaurants, and shop for groceries at supermarkets and the green market.

**Base Language:** English

## 1. Overview

Welcome to Unit 3! In this unit, you will learn how to navigate two essential aspects of daily life in Serbian-speaking regions: eating out and shopping for food. You'll learn vocabulary for ordering at cafés and restaurants (konoba), as well as shopping at supermarkets and the traditional green market (zelena pijaca).

#### A Note from the Founder (Jacksenn)

> "Coffee culture is huge in Montenegro and Serbia—people spend hours at cafés, and it's a central part of social life. Equally important is the green market (pijaca), where you'll find the freshest produce and have real conversations with local vendors. These two places—the café and the market—are where you'll truly feel connected to daily life here. Don't be shy about ordering or asking for what you need. Even if you make mistakes, people will appreciate your effort and often help you along the way."

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this unit, you will be able to:
- Order food and drinks at a café or restaurant

- Shop for groceries at a supermarket
- Buy fruits and vegetables at the green market

## 3. Grammar (Gramatika)

### A. Verb "hteti" (to want)

"Hteti" is an irregular verb used to express wants and desires. It is essential for ordering food and shopping.

**Full Conjugation:**

| Person | Full Form | Short Form (Common) | English |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ja | hoću | ću | I want |
| Ti | hoćeš | ćeš | You want (informal) |
| On/Ona/Ono | hoće | će | He/She/It wants |
| Mi | hoćemo | ćemo | We want |
| Vi | hoćete | ćete | You want (formal/plural) |
| Oni/One/Ona | hoće | će | They want |

**Negative Form:**

| Person | Negative | English |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ja | neću | I don't want |
| Ti | nećeš | You don't want |
| On/Ona/Ono | neće | He/She/It doesn't want |
| Mi | nećemo | We don't want |
| Vi | nećete | You don't want |
| Oni/One/Ona | neće | They don't want |

**Examples:**
- Hoću kafu. (I want coffee.)
- Hoćeš li vodu? (Do you want water?)
- Ne hoću pivo. (I don't want beer.)
- Hoćemo salatu. (We want salad.)

**Note:** The short forms (ću, ćeš, će) are very common in spoken Serbian and are often used to form the future tense.

### B. Verb "želeti" (to wish/want - polite)

"Želeti" is a more polite way to express wants and desires, often used in formal situations or restaurants.

**Full Conjugation:**

| Person | Form | English |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ja | želim | I wish/want |
| Ti | želiš | You wish/want |
| On/Ona/Ono | želi | He/She/It wishes/wants |
| Mi | želimo | We wish/want |
| Vi | želite | You wish/want (formal/plural) |
| Oni/One/Ona | žele | They wish/want |

**Examples:**
- Želim kafu. (I would like coffee.)
- Šta želite? (What would you like?)
- Želimo račun. (We would like the bill.)

### C. Verb "imati" (to have)

"Imati" is used to express possession and is essential for asking if something is available.

**Full Conjugation:**

| Person | Form | English |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ja | imam | I have |
| Ti | imaš | You have |
| On/Ona/Ono | ima | He/She/It has |
| Mi | imamo | We have |
| Vi | imate | You have (formal/plural) |
| Oni/One/Ona | imaju | They have |

**Negative Form:**

| Person | Negative | English |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ja | nemam | I don't have |
| Ti | nemaš | You don't have |
| On/Ona/Ono | nema | He/She/It doesn't have |
| Mi | nemamo | We don't have |
| Vi | nemate | You don't have |
| Oni/One/Ona | nemaju | They don't have |

**Examples:**
- Imam jabuku. (I have an apple.)
- Imate li hleb? (Do you have bread?)
- Nemamo sir. (We don't have cheese.)

**Note:** "Imate li...?" is the standard way to ask "Do you have...?" in shops and restaurants.

### D. Basic Accusative Case (Introduction)

In Serbian, the accusative case is used for direct objects (the thing being acted upon). For now, you only need to recognize it in common food-related contexts.

**Basic Pattern:**
- Masculine nouns: Often add nothing or change the ending
  - hleb → hleb (Hoću hleb. - I want bread.)
- Feminine nouns ending in -a: Change -a to -u
  - kafa → kafu (Hoću kafu. - I want coffee.)
  - voda → vodu (Hoću vodu. - I want water.)
- Neuter nouns: Usually no change

**Examples:**
- Hoću kafu. (I want coffee.) - kafa → kafu
- Hoću vodu. (I want water.) - voda → vodu
- Hoću hleb. (I want bread.) - hleb → hleb (no change)

**Note:** Don't worry about memorizing all the rules now. You'll learn more about cases in later units. For now, just recognize the pattern in the phrases you're learning.

### Dialogue 1: Ordering at a Café

**Konobar:** Dobar dan! Šta želite?  
(Good day! What would you like?)

**Alex:** Dobar dan. Hoću kafu, molim.  
(Good day. I want coffee, please.)

**Konobar:** Espreso ili tursku kafu?  
(Espresso or Turkish coffee?)

**Alex:** Tursku kafu.  
(Turkish coffee.)

**Konobar:** Izvolite. Još nešto?  
(Here you are. Anything else?)

**Alex:** Ne, hvala.  
(No, thank you.)

---

### Dialogue 2: At the Green Market

**Alex:** Dobar dan. Koliko košta paradajz?  
(Good day. How much do tomatoes cost?)

**Prodavac:** Sto dinara kilo.  
(One hundred dinars per kilo.)

**Alex:** Hoću dva kila, molim.  
(I want two kilos, please.)

**Prodavac:** Izvolite. Još nešto?  
(Here you are. Anything else?)

**Alex:** Imate li krastavac?  
(Do you have cucumbers?)

**Prodavac:** Da, imam. Pedeset dinara kilo.  
(Yes, I do. Fifty dinars per kilo.)

**Alex:** Hoću jedan kilo. Hvala.  
(I want one kilo. Thank you.)

**Prodavac:** Molim. Prijatno!  
(You're welcome. Enjoy!)

---

### Dialogue 3: At the Supermarket

**Alex:** Izvinite, gde je hleb?  
(Excuse me, where is the bread?)

**Prodavačica:** Tamo, levo.  
(There, on the left.)

**Alex:** Hvala. Imate li mleko?  
(Thank you. Do you have milk?)

**Prodavačica:** Da, u frižideru.  
(Yes, in the refrigerator.)

**Alex:** Hvala mnogo.  
(Thank you very much.)

---

### Dialogue 4: Asking for the Bill

**Alex:** Izvinite, račun molim.  
(Excuse me, the bill please.)

**Konobar:** Izvolite. Hiljadu dinara.  
(Here you are. One thousand dinars.)

**Alex:** Izvolite. Hvala.  
(Here you are. Thank you.)

**Konobar:** Molim. Prijatno!  
(You're welcome. Enjoy!)

## 6. Cultural Note

### Food Culture in Serbia and Montenegro

**Coffee Culture:**
Coffee (kafa) is central to social life in Serbia and Montenegro. People spend hours at cafés (kafići), and it's common to meet friends for coffee multiple times a day. Turkish coffee (turska kafa) is traditional, but espresso and cappuccino are also very popular.

**The Green Market (Zelena Pijaca):**
The green market is where locals buy fresh fruits, vegetables, and other produce. It's a vibrant social space where you can practice your Serbian and get the freshest ingredients. Prices are usually per kilogram, and vendors are often friendly and willing to help.

**Restaurant Etiquette:**
- Service can be slower than in Western countries—meals are meant to be enjoyed, not rushed.
- Tipping is appreciated but not mandatory. 10% is generous.
- "Prijatno!" is said before eating, similar to "Bon appétit!"
- "Živeli!" is the toast when drinking together.

**Montenegrin Variants:**
- **Mleko** (milk) → **Mlijeko** in Montenegro
- **Hleb** (bread) → **Hljeb** in Montenegro
