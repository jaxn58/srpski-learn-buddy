## 3. Grammar (Gramatika)

### A. Verb "biti" (to be) - Essential Forms

The verb "biti" (to be) is one of the most important verbs in Serbian. In this unit, we learn three basic forms.

#### Affirmative Forms

| Person | Serbian | English | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ja | sam | I am | Ja sam turista. (I am a tourist.) |
| Ti | si | You are (informal) | Ti si iz Engleske. (You are from England.) |
| On/Ona | je | He/She is | On je konobar. (He is a waiter.) |

#### Negative Forms

| Person | Serbian | English | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ja | nisam | I am not | Ja nisam iz Beograda. (I am not from Belgrade.) |
| Ti | nisi | You are not | Ti nisi turista? (You are not a tourist?) |
| On/Ona | nije | He/She is not | On nije ovde. (He is not here.) |

**Note:** In Unit 2, you will learn the complete conjugation of "biti" with all six persons (ja, ti, on/ona, mi, vi, oni/one).

---

### B. Gender in Serbian (Rod) - Introduction

Serbian nouns have three genders: **masculine (m)**, **feminine (f)**, and **neuter (n)**. The gender is usually indicated by the word's ending.

| Gender | Typical Ending | Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Masculine (m)** | Consonant | konobar, student, hotel, pasoš, taksi |
| **Feminine (f)** | -a | turistkinja, konobarica, Srbija, Engleska |
| **Neuter (n)** | -o, -e | ime (name) |

**Why is gender important?**

Gender affects how adjectives and verbs agree with nouns. In this unit, we mark gender only for people (turista/turistkinja, konobar/konobarica) because it's essential to know which form to use when talking about yourself or others.

**Note:** You don't need to memorize all gender rules now. As you progress, you will naturally learn to recognize patterns.

### B. Dialogue 1: At the Border (Na granici)

**Scene:** Alex arrives at a border crossing.

| Role | Serbian | English |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Border Officer** | Dobar dan. Pasoš, molim. | Good day. Passport, please. |
| **Alex** | Dobar dan. Izvolite. | Good day. Here you are. |
| **Border Officer** | Hvala. Odakle ste? | Thank you. Where are you from? |
| **Alex** | Ja sam iz Engleske. | I am from England. |
| **Border Officer** | Dobro. Hvala. | Good. Thank you. |
| **Alex** | Hvala. Doviđenja. | Thank you. Goodbye. |

---

### C. Dialogue 2: Taking a Taxi (U taksiju)

**Scene:** Alex takes a taxi from the airport.

| Role | Serbian | English |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Alex** | Zdravo! Hotel, molim. | Hello! Hotel, please. |
| **Taxi Driver** | Dobar dan. Koji hotel? | Good day. Which hotel? |
| **Alex** | Hotel "Centar". | Hotel "Centar". |
| **Taxi Driver** | Dobro. Govorite li srpski? | Good. Do you speak Serbian? |
| **Alex** | Ne, ne govorim. Govorim engleski. | No, I don't speak. I speak English. |
| **Taxi Driver** | U redu. Ja govorim malo engleski. | Alright. I speak a little English. |
| **Alex** | Odlično! Hvala. | Excellent! Thank you. |

**Note:** In Montenegro, "Govorite" would be pronounced "Govorite" (same), but "Ne razumem" would be "Ne razumijem."

## Cultural Note: Greetings and Politeness in Serbian Culture

In Serbian culture, **greetings** are very important. When entering a shop, restaurant, or any public space, it's customary to greet people with **"Dobar dan"** (Good day) or **"Dobro veče"** (Good evening).

**Formality:** Serbians use **formal language** when meeting someone for the first time or in professional settings. Always use **"Vi"** (formal "you") until the other person suggests switching to **"Ti"** (informal "you").

**Hospitality:** Serbians are known for their warm hospitality. Don't be surprised if someone invites you for coffee or rakija (traditional brandy) shortly after meeting you!

**"Molim" - A Versatile Word:** The word **"molim"** has multiple meanings depending on context:
- **Please** (when asking for something)
- **You're welcome** (response to "thank you")
- **Pardon?** (when you didn't hear something)

**Montenegrin Variants:** In Montenegro, some words are pronounced differently:
- **Razumem** (I understand) → **Razumijem**
- **Ne razumem** (I don't understand) → **Ne razumijem**
- **Gde** (Where) → **Gdje**
- **Ovde** (Here) → **Ovdje**

These are pronunciation differences, not different words. Both forms are understood throughout the region.
