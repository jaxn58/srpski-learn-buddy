# Module 1: The Arrival
## Unit 1: First Words

**Base Language:** English
**Target Language:** Serbian

## 1. Overview

This unit provides the essential survival phrases for your first moments in Serbia or Montenegro. We focus on greetings, basic politeness, and overcoming the language barrier.

**Learning Objectives:**

By the end of this unit, you will be able to:

1. Greet people appropriately in formal and informal situations
2. Use basic politeness expressions (thank you, please, excuse me)
3. Say yes and no confidently
4. Explain that you don't speak Serbian and ask if someone speaks English
5. Introduce yourself with your name

## 3. Grammar (Gramatika)

### A. Verb "biti" (to be) - Essential Forms

The verb **"biti"** (to be) is the most important verb in Serbian. In this unit, we learn three forms in affirmative and negative.

#### Conjugation Table

| Person | Affirmative | Negative |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ja (I) | sam | nisam |
| Ti (You, informal) | si | nisi |
| On/Ona (He/She) | je | nije |

**Affirmative Examples:**
- Ja sam Alex. (I am Alex.)
- Ti si turista. (You are a tourist.)
- On je konobar. (He is a waiter.)
- Ona je turistkinja. (She is a tourist.)

**Negative Examples:**
- Ja nisam iz Srbije. (I am not from Serbia.)
- Ti nisi turista? (You are not a tourist?)
- On nije ovde. (He is not here.)
- Ona nije konobarica. (She is not a waitress.)

**Important:** The negative forms (nisam, nisi, nije) are written as ONE word, not two.

---

### B. Gender in Serbian (Rod)

Serbian nouns have three genders: **masculine**, **feminine**, and **neuter**. You can usually tell the gender by the word ending:

| Gender | Typical Ending | Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Masculine (m)** | Consonant | konobar, student, hotel, pasoš |
| **Feminine (f)** | -a | turistkinja, konobarica, Srbija |
| **Neuter (n)** | -o, -e | ime (name) |

**Why is gender important?** Gender affects how adjectives and verbs agree with nouns. In this unit, we mark gender only for people (turista/turistkinja, konobar/konobarica) because it's essential to know which form to use when talking about yourself or others.

---

### C. Verb "govoriti" (to speak) - First Person Only

For this unit, we only need the first person singular: **govorim** (I speak).

**Affirmative:**
- Govorim engleski. (I speak English.)

**Negative:**
- Ne govorim srpski. (I don't speak Serbian.)

**Rule:** To make a verb negative, add **"ne"** before the verb (written as two words).

### B. Dialogue 1: At the Border (Na granici)

**Scene:** Alex arrives at the Serbian border for passport control.

| Role | Serbian | English |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Officer** | Dobar dan. Pasoš, molim. | Good day. Passport, please. |
| **Alex** | Dobar dan. Izvolite. | Good day. Here you are. |
| **Officer** | Hvala. Turista? | Thank you. Tourist? |
| **Alex** | Da, turista. | Yes, tourist. |
| **Officer** | Dobro. Doviđenja. | Good. Goodbye. |
| **Alex** | Doviđenja. | Goodbye. |

---

### C. Dialogue 2: In the Taxi (U taksiju)

**Scene:** Alex gets into a taxi and needs to communicate.

| Role | Serbian | English |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Driver** | Dobar dan. Gde? | Good day. Where to? |
| **Alex** | Izvinite, ne govorim srpski. Govorite li engleski? | Excuse me, I don't speak Serbian. Do you speak English? |
| **Driver** | Da, malo. Hotel? | Yes, a little. Hotel? |
| **Alex** | Da, hotel. Hvala. | Yes, hotel. Thank you. |
| **Driver** | Molim. | You're welcome. |

**Montenegro Note:** Use **Gdje** instead of **Gde**.

### E. Dialogue 4: Meeting Someone (Upoznavanje)

**Scene:** Alex meets a local person at a café.

| Role | Serbian | English |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Local** | Zdravo! | Hello! |
| **Alex** | Zdravo! Ja sam Alex. | Hello! I am Alex. |
| **Local** | Drago mi je. Ja sam Marko. Ti si turista? | Nice to meet you. I am Marko. Are you a tourist? |
| **Alex** | Da, ja sam turista. | Yes, I am a tourist. |
| **Local** | Govorite li srpski? | Do you speak Serbian? |
| **Alex** | Ne, ne govorim srpski. Govorim engleski. | No, I don't speak Serbian. I speak English. |
| **Local** | Razumem. | I understand. |
| **Alex** | Hvala! | Thank you! |

**Montenegro Note:** Use **Razumijem** instead of **Razumem**.

## Cultural Note: Greetings in Serbian Culture

**Greetings are essential:** In Serbia and Montenegro, greeting people is a sign of respect. Always say "Dobar dan" when entering a shop, restaurant, or office.

**"Molim" is versatile:** This word means both "please" and "you're welcome." It's one of the most useful words in Serbian.

**Always say goodbye:** It's considered rude to leave without saying "Doviđenja." Serbians and Montenegrins value proper farewells.

**Smiles help:** Even if your pronunciation isn't perfect, a smile and an attempt to speak Serbian will be warmly received.

**Montenegrin Variants:** In Montenegro, the Ijekavian dialect is used. Key differences: **Razumijem** (not Razumem), **Gdje** (not Gde), **Ovdje** (not Ovde). Both variants are understood and accepted throughout the region.
