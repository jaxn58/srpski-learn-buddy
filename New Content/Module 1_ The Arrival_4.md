## 1. Overview

Welcome to your first unit! This unit focuses on the absolute essentials you need for your first day in Serbia or Montenegro: greetings, basic politeness, and simple communication phrases.

#### A Note from the Founder (Jacksenn)

> "Let's be honest: at the beginning, learning a new language can be incredibly frustrating. It's hard to remember vocabulary, and you often feel like you'll never understand how to speak it. You might feel insecure in a restaurant or hesitate to use what you've learned. But here is our promise: Do not put pressure on yourself. You must have the courage to simply try. When people see that you are making an effort to speak their language, you will always bring a smile to their face. We are here to give you that courage."

#### Learning Objectives

By the end of this unit, you will be able to:

1. Greet people appropriately at different times of day
2. Say thank you, please, and excuse me politely
3. Introduce yourself with your name
4. Ask if someone speaks English
5. Say you don't understand or don't speak Serbian
6. Use basic yes/no responses

## 3. Grammar (Gramatika)

### A. Verb "biti" (to be) - Essential Forms

The verb **"biti"** (to be) is the most important verb in Serbian. For Unit 1, we focus on three essential forms.

#### Basic Forms

| Person | Serbian | English | Negative |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ja | sam | I am | nisam |
| Ti | si | You are (informal) | nisi |
| On/Ona | je | He/She is | nije |

**Examples:**
- Ja sam Alex. (I am Alex.)
- Ti si iz Srbije? (Are you from Serbia?)
- On je turista. (He is a tourist.)
- Ja nisam iz Engleske. (I am not from England.)
- Ti nisi ovde. (You are not here.)
- Ona nije konobarica. (She is not a waitress.)

**Note:** We will learn the full conjugation (Mi, Vi, Oni) in Unit 2.

---

### B. Gender in Serbian (Rod) - Introduction

Serbian nouns have three genders: **masculine**, **feminine**, and **neuter**. The gender affects how adjectives and verbs agree with nouns.

#### Gender and Word Endings

| Gender | Typical Ending | Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Masculine (m)** | Consonant | turista, hotel, pasoš, ključ |
| **Feminine (f)** | -a | turistkinja, konobarica |
| **Neuter (n)** | -o, -e | ime (name) |

**Why is gender important?**
Gender affects how we use adjectives, verbs, and pronouns. For now, we mark gender only for people (turista/turistkinja, konobar/konobarica) because it's essential to know which form to use when talking about yourself or others.

---

### C. Verb "govoriti" (to speak) - First Person Only

The verb **"govoriti"** (to speak) is essential for communication. For Unit 1, we focus on the first person only.

| Person | Serbian | English | Negative |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ja | govorim | I speak | ne govorim |

**Examples:**
- Ja govorim engleski. (I speak English.)
- Ja ne govorim srpski. (I don't speak Serbian.)

**Question Form:**
- Govorite li engleski? (Do you speak English? - formal)

**Note:** We will learn the full conjugation in Unit 2.

### B. Dialogue 1: At the Border (Na granici)

**Scene:** Alex arrives at the Serbian border.

| Role | Serbian | English |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Border Officer** | Dobar dan. Pasoš, molim vas. | Good day. Passport, please. |
| **Alex** | Dobar dan. Izvolite. | Good day. Here you are. |
| **Border Officer** | Hvala. Odakle ste? | Thank you. Where are you from? |
| **Alex** | Ja sam iz Engleske. | I am from England. |
| **Border Officer** | U redu. Hvala. | Alright. Thank you. |
| **Alex** | Hvala vam. Doviđenja. | Thank you. Goodbye. |

**Montenegro Note:** Use **Gdje** instead of **Gde** in "Odakle ste?".

---

### C. Dialogue 2: In a Taxi (U taksiju)

**Scene:** Alex takes a taxi from the airport.

| Role | Serbian | English |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Taxi Driver** | Dobar dan! Gde? | Good day! Where to? |
| **Alex** | Dobar dan. Hotel, molim vas. | Good day. Hotel, please. |
| **Taxi Driver** | U redu. Govorite li srpski? | Alright. Do you speak Serbian? |
| **Alex** | Ne, ne govorim srpski. Govorite li engleski? | No, I don't speak Serbian. Do you speak English? |
| **Taxi Driver** | Da, malo. | Yes, a little. |
| **Alex** | Hvala! | Thank you! |

**Montenegro Note:** Use **Gdje** instead of **Gde**.

## Cultural Note: Formality and Greetings in Serbian Culture

**Formal vs. Informal:** Serbians and Montenegrins distinguish clearly between formal (Vi) and informal (Ti) address. When meeting someone for the first time, especially in professional settings, always use the formal "Vi" form until invited to use "Ti".

**Time-Specific Greetings:** Unlike English, Serbian has specific greetings for different times of day:
- **Dobro jutro** (Good morning) - until noon
- **Dobar dan** (Good day) - anytime, but especially afternoon
- **Dobro veče** (Good evening) - after 6 PM
- **Laku noć** (Good night) - only before sleep

**"Molim" is Versatile:** The word "Molim" can mean "please," "you're welcome," or "pardon?" depending on context. Listen for the tone and situation.

**Effort is Appreciated:** When you make an effort to speak Serbian, even just a few words, locals will appreciate it immensely. Don't worry about making mistakes—the effort itself brings smiles.

**Montenegrin Variants:** In Montenegro, some words have Ijekavian pronunciation:
- **Razumijem** instead of **Razumem** (I understand)
- **Gdje** instead of **Gde** (where)
- **Ovdje** instead of **Ovde** (here)

Both variants are understood throughout the region.
