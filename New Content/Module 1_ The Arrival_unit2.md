# Module 1: The Arrival
## Unit 2: Who Are You?

**Base Language:** English
**Target Language:** Serbian

---

## 1. Overview

This unit focuses on personal identity and introductions. You will learn to say your name, ask others their names, state where you're from, and discuss nationalities and professions.

**Learning Objectives:**

By the end of this unit, you will be able to:

1. Introduce yourself with your full name and origin
2. Ask someone their name and where they're from
3. Use the complete conjugation of the verb "biti" (to be)
4. Use the complete conjugation of the verb "zvati se" (to be called)
5. Name countries and nationalities in Serbian
6. Discuss basic professions and status

## 3. Grammar (Gramatika)

### A. Verb "biti" (to be) - Complete Conjugation

In Unit 1, we learned three forms of "biti". Now we learn all six forms.

#### Full Conjugation Table

| Person | Affirmative | Negative |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ja (I) | sam | nisam |
| Ti (You, informal) | si | nisi |
| On/Ona/Ono (He/She/It) | je | nije |
| Mi (We) | smo | nismo |
| Vi (You, formal/plural) | ste | niste |
| Oni/One/Ona (They) | su | nisu |

**Examples:**
- Mi smo iz Nemačke. (We are from Germany.)
- Vi ste turisti? (Are you tourists?)
- Oni su studenti. (They are students.)
- Mi nismo iz Srbije. (We are not from Serbia.)
- Vi niste Englezi? (You are not English?)
- Oni nisu ovde. (They are not here.)

---

### B. Verb "zvati se" (to be called) - Complete Conjugation

The verb **"zvati se"** is reflexive and means "to be called" or "to call oneself".

#### Full Conjugation Table

| Person | Serbian | English |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ja | zovem se | I am called / My name is |
| Ti | zoveš se | You are called (informal) |
| On/Ona | zove se | He/She is called |
| Mi | zovemo se | We are called |
| Vi | zovete se | You are called (formal/plural) |
| Oni/One | zovu se | They are called |

**Examples:**
- Ja se zovem Alex. (My name is Alex.)
- Kako se zoveš? (What's your name? - informal)
- Kako se zovete? (What's your name? - formal)
- On se zove Marko. (His name is Marko.)
- Mi se zovemo Smith. (Our name is Smith.)

**Important:** The reflexive pronoun "se" can come before or after the verb, but for beginners, we recommend placing it after the verb.

### C. Nationalities and Gender Agreement

Nationalities in Serbian change based on gender. The masculine form typically ends in a consonant or -ac, while the feminine form typically ends in -a or -kinja.

| Masculine | Feminine | Country |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Nemac | Nemica | Nemačka |
| Englez | Engleskinja | Engleska |
| Amerikanac | Amerikanka | Amerika |
| Srbin | Srpkinja | Srbija |
| Crnogorac | Crnogorka | Crna Gora |

**Examples:**
- Ja sam Nemac. (I am German - male speaker)
- Ja sam Nemica. (I am German - female speaker)
- On je Englez. (He is English.)
- Ona je Engleskinja. (She is English.)

### B. Dialogue 1: First Meeting (Prvo upoznavanje)

**Scene:** Alex meets Marko at a café in Belgrade.

| Role | Serbian | English |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Marko** | Zdravo! Ja sam Marko. Kako se zoveš? | Hello! I am Marko. What's your name? |
| **Alex** | Zdravo! Ja sam Alex. Drago mi je. | Hello! I am Alex. Nice to meet you. |
| **Marko** | Drago mi je. Odakle si? | Nice to meet you. Where are you from? |
| **Alex** | Ja sam iz Engleske. Ti? | I am from England. You? |
| **Marko** | Ja sam iz Srbije, iz Beograda. | I am from Serbia, from Belgrade. |
| **Alex** | Lepo! | Nice! |

---

### C. Dialogue 2: Formal Introduction (Formalno upoznavanje)

**Scene:** Alex meets a business contact at a hotel.

| Role | Serbian | English |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Contact** | Dobar dan. Kako se zovete? | Good day. What's your name? |
| **Alex** | Dobar dan. Zovem se Alex Smith. | Good day. My name is Alex Smith. |
| **Contact** | Drago mi je. Ja sam Petar Jovanović. Odakle ste? | Nice to meet you. I am Petar Jovanović. Where are you from? |
| **Alex** | Ja sam iz Engleske, iz Londona. | I am from England, from London. |
| **Contact** | Dobrodošli u Srbiju! | Welcome to Serbia! |
| **Alex** | Hvala! | Thank you! |

## Cultural Note: Names and Formality in Serbian Culture

**Formal vs. Informal:** Serbians and Montenegrins distinguish clearly between formal (Vi) and informal (Ti) address. When meeting someone for the first time, especially in professional settings, always use the formal "Vi" form until invited to use "Ti".

**Full Names:** When introducing yourself formally, give both your first name (ime) and last name (prezime). Example: "Zovem se Alex Smith."

**Patronymics:** While not as common as in Russian, some Serbian last names are patronymic (based on the father's name), ending in -ović (son of) or -ević.

**Hospitality:** When someone says "Dobrodošli!" (Welcome!), it's a genuine expression of hospitality. Serbians and Montenegrins take pride in welcoming guests.

**Montenegrin Variants:** In Montenegro, use **Ljekar** / **Ljekarka** instead of **Lekar** / **Lekarka** for "doctor". Both variants are understood throughout the region.
