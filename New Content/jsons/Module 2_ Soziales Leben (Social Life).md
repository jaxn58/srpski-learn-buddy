## 1. Overview

#### A Note from the Founder (Jacksenn)

> "After settling in, the next step is building a social life. I remember my first time going out with local friends—trying to understand when and where to meet, what to order, and how to keep up with the conversation. It was challenging, but also incredibly rewarding. This unit gives you the tools to make plans, meet friends, and enjoy the vibrant café and restaurant culture of Serbia and Montenegro. Trust me, once you can say 'Hajde da se nađemo večeras,' you're no longer just a tourist—you're part of the community."

#### Learning Objectives

By the end of this unit, **we** will be able to:

1. **Make plans with friends** using time expressions and days of the week.
2. **Tell and understand time** (What time is it? At what time?).
3. **Use the verb "moći" (can)** to ask if someone is available.
4. **Use the verb "piti" (to drink)** to order drinks and socialize.
5. **Make suggestions** using "Hajde da..." (Let's...).
6. **Navigate conversations** in cafés and restaurants.
7. **Understand the social culture** of rakija, coffee, and dining out.

## 3. Grammar (Gramatika)

### A. Days of the Week - Planning Ahead

The days of the week are essential for making plans. In Serbian, the week starts on Monday.

#### Days of the Week

| Serbian | English |
| :--- | :--- |
| Ponedeljak* | Monday |
| Utorak | Tuesday |
| Sreda | Wednesday |
| Četvrtak* | Thursday |
| Petak | Friday |
| Subota | Saturday |
| Nedelja* | Sunday |

*Montenegro: Ponedeljak, Četvrtak, Nedelja (same)

#### Using Days in Sentences

To say "**on Monday**," use **"u ponedeljak"** (literally: "in Monday").

**Examples:**
*   **U ponedeljak idem u grad.** (On Monday I'm going to town.)
*   **Možeš li u petak?** (Can you on Friday?)
*   **Vidimo se u subotu.** (See you on Saturday.)

**Note:** The word "sat" (hour) changes form:
*   1 sat
*   2-4 sata

#### Half Hours

To say "half past," use **"pola"** (half) + the **next hour**.

**Examples:**
*   **Pola šest** = 5:30 (literally: "half six")
*   **Pola osam** = 7:30 (literally: "half eight")

### C. Verb "moći" (can / to be able to)

The verb **"moći"** is essential for asking if someone is available or able to do something.

#### Conjugation Table

| Person | Serbian | English |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ja** | mogu | I can |
| **Ti** | možeš | You can (informal) |
| **On/Ona/Ono** | može | He/She/It can |
| **Mi** | možemo | We can |
| **Vi** | možete | You can (formal/plural) |
| **Oni/One/Ona** | mogu | They can |

#### Practical Examples

*   **Mogu li da dođem?** (Can I come?)
*   **Možeš li večeras?** (Can you tonight?)
*   **Ne mogu sutra.** (I can't tomorrow.)
*   **Možemo da se nađemo.** (We can meet.)
*   **Ona može u petak.** (She can on Friday.)

## C. Cultural Note: The Social Culture of Serbia and Montenegro

**Rakija Culture:** Rakija is not just a drink—it's a social ritual and a symbol of hospitality. When someone offers you rakija, it's a sign of friendship and trust. Always say "Nazdravlje!" (Cheers) before drinking, and make eye contact.

**Coffee Culture:** Coffee (kafa) is the backbone of social life. Serbians and Montenegrins can spend hours at a café over a single cup of coffee. It's not about the drink—it's about the conversation and connection.

**"Kafana" vs. "Kafić":** A "kafana" is a traditional Serbian tavern, often with live music and a lively atmosphere. A "kafić" is a modern café, usually quieter and more relaxed. Both are essential to social life.

**Splitting the Bill:** In Serbia and Montenegro, it's common for one person to pay the entire bill as a gesture of generosity. If someone insists on paying, don't argue too much—accept graciously and offer to pay next time.

**Being Late:** Punctuality is more relaxed in the Balkans. If you're 10-15 minutes late, it's usually not a big deal. Just say "Izvinite što kasnim" (Sorry I'm late) and move on.
