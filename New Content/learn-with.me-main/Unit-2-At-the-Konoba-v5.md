## 3. Grammar (Gramatika)

### A. The Verb "hteti" / "htjeti" (to want) - Our Ordering Tool

The verb "hteti" (Montenegro: **"htjeti"**) is our most important tool for ordering. It is irregular and often shortened in speech, which is why **we** need to learn its full and short forms.

**Montenegrin Note:** In Montenegro, this verb is spelled "htjeti" (Ijekavian), but the conjugated forms (hoću, hoćeš, etc.) remain the same.

#### Conjugation Table: Hteti / Htjeti (to want)

| Pronoun | Full Form | Short Form | English |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ja | hoću | ću | I want |
| Ti | hoćeš | ćeš | You want (Informal) |
| On/Ona/Ono | hoće | će | He/She/It wants |
| Mi | hoćemo | ćemo | We want |
| Vi | hoćete | ćete | You want (Formal/Plural) |
| Oni/One/Ona | hoće | će | They want |

#### How to Use "hteti" / "htjeti" for Ordering

**We** will almost always use the **Short Form** (ću, ćeš, će, etc.) when ordering, and it always comes after the first word in the sentence.

*   **Polite Ordering (The best way):** Use the full verb **"Želim"** (I wish/I want) from the verb *želeti*. This is the most polite way to order.
    *   *Example:* **Želim** jednu kafu, molim vas. (I want one coffee, please.)
*   **Simple Ordering (The common way):** Use the short form **"ću"** with the word **"Ja"** (I).
    *   *Example:* Ja **ću** pivo. (I will have a beer.)

#### Negative Form of "hteti" / "htjeti" (to not want)

The negative form is simple and regular: **neću, nećeš, neće,** etc.

| Pronoun | Negative Form | English |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ja | neću | I don't want |
| Ti | nećeš | You don't want |
| On/Ona/Ono | neće | He/She/It doesn't want |
| Mi | nećemo | We don't want |
| Vi | nećete | You don't want |
| Oni/One/Ona | neće | They don't want |

**Example:** **Neću** hleb, hvala. (I don't want bread, thank you.)

### B. Numbers 1-10 (Brojevi) - Specifying Quantity

To order correctly, **we** need to specify how many of something **we** want.

| Number | Serbian (Ekavian) | Serbian (Ijekavian) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | jedan (m.), jedna (f.), jedno (n.) | jedan (m.), jedna (f.), jedno (n.) |
| 2 | dva (m.), dve (f.) | dva (m.), dvije (f.) |
| 3 | tri | tri |
| 4 | četiri | četiri |
| 5 | pet | pet |
| 6 | šest | šest |
| 7 | sedam | sedam |
| 8 | osam | osam |
| 9 | devet | devet |
| 10 | deset | deset |

**Note on Gender:** The number 1 is an adjective and must match the gender of the noun (e.g., *jedan* hleb, *jedna* kafa, *jedno* pivo). The number 2 also has gendered forms. **We** will focus on the most common ones for now.

#### Practical Examples with Numbers

| Serbian | English | Explanation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Jednu** kafu, molim. | **One** coffee, please. | Kafa is feminine, so we use *jednu*. |
| **Dva** piva, molim. | **Two** beers, please. | Pivo is neuter, so we use *dva*. |
| **Tri** salate. | **Three** salads. | Numbers 3-10 are simpler. |

### C. Cultural Note: The Konoba and Ijekavian Variants

*   **Konoba:** A traditional tavern or restaurant, often with a rustic feel, especially common in coastal areas like Montenegro. It's a place for local food and a relaxed atmosphere.
*   **Ijekavian Variants:** In Montenegro, you will hear "Hljeb" instead of "Hleb" (bread). Remember that the core grammar remains the same, but the pronunciation of certain words changes. **We** are preparing **you** for both!
*   **Tipping:** Tipping is customary, usually around 10% if the service was good. Leaving the change is common.
