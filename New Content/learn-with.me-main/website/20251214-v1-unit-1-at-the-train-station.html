3. Grammar (Gramatika)

A. The Verbs "biti" (to be) and "zvati se" (to be called)

These are the foundation of self-introduction, and we will master them first.

Conjugation Table: Biti (to be) - The Core Tool

Verb Pronoun Conjugation English
biti (to be) Ja sam / jesam I am
Ti si / jesi You are (Informal)
On/Ona/Ono je / jeste He/She/It is
Mi smo / jesmo We are
Vi ste / jeste You are (Formal/Plural)
Oni/One/Ona su / jesu They are

Short Form vs. Long Form: Why Two Words?

For beginners, the most important thing we need to know is:

  1. Short Form (sam, si, je, etc.) is the Standard Form used in almost all everyday sentences.
    • Example: Ja sam Alex. (I am Alex.)
  2. Long Form (jesam, jesi, jeste, etc.) is used for:
    • Emphasis: To strongly confirm something. (e.g., "Yes, I am a tourist!")
    • Questions with "li": This is the most common use for beginners, as explained in the "Asking Simple Questions" section. (e.g., Jesi li ti Alex?)

B. Gender of Nouns (Rod imenica)

Serbian nouns have three genders. We will see that this is crucial for agreement with adjectives, pronouns, and verb forms.

Gender Rules

Gender Typical Ending Example English
Masculine (m.) Consonant voz train
Feminine (f.) -a karta ticket
Neuter (n.) -o or -e selo village

Why Gender Matters

Gender affects how adjectives and demonstratives change. For example:

Gender Noun Adjective "good" Full Phrase English
Masculine voz dobar dobar voz good train
Feminine karta dobra dobra karta good ticket
Neuter selo dobro dobro selo good village

Practical Examples in Context

Serbian English Explanation
Gde je voz? Where is the train? "Voz" is masculine (ends in consonant)
Karta je skupa. The ticket is expensive. "Karta" is feminine (ends in -a)
Selo je lepo. The village is beautiful. "Selo" is neuter (ends in -o)
Ovo je moj voz. This is my train. "Moj" (my) is masculine form
Ovo je moja karta. This is my ticket. "Moja" (my) is feminine form
Ovo je moje selo. This is my village. "Moje" (my) is neuter form

C. Asking Simple Questions (The "li" Particle)

In Serbian, we can turn a statement into a simple Yes/No question by adding the particle "li" after the first verb in the sentence. We will see that this is a crucial difference from English.

Rule

Statement: Subject + Verb (Short Form) + Object Question: Verb (Long Form) + li + Subject + Object

Note: When forming a question with "li", you must use the Long Form of the verb "biti" (jesam, jesi, jeste, etc.) or the full verb for others (zove li).

Examples

Statement (Serbian) Question (Serbian) English Translation
Ti si turista. Jesi li ti turista? Are you a tourist?
Voz je ovde. Je li voz ovde? Is the train here?
Vi ste Alex. Jeste li vi Alex? Are you Alex?
On se zove Marko. Zove li se on Marko? Is he called Marko?
Vi niste iz Crne Gore. Niste li vi iz Crne Gore? Aren't you from Montenegro? (Negative Question)

D. Question Words (Pitanja)

For questions that require more than Yes/No, you use question words (Pitanja).

Serbian English Example
Gde Where Gde je peron? (Where is the platform?)
Kako How Kako se zovete? (What is your name?)
Šta What Šta je ovo? (What is this?)

C. Cultural Note: Serbian vs. Montenegrin Usage

This section addresses your specific location to make the content more relevant and authentic.

  • Ekavian vs. Ijekavian: Serbian uses the Ekavian dialect as the standard, which uses "e" where Montenegrin/Bosnian/Croatian (Ijekavian) uses "ije" or "je".
    • Serbia (Ekavian - Primary): železnička (train station), mleko (milk)
    • Montenegro (Ijekavian - Variant): željeznička (train station), mlijeko (milk)
    • Strategic Advice: We teach Serbian (Ekavian) as the primary language. Montenegrin variants are provided as side-notes to help us adapt to the local dialect when in Montenegro.
  • Formal Address: Always use "Vi" (formal You) with strangers, service personnel, and elders. The informal "Ti" is reserved for friends and family.
