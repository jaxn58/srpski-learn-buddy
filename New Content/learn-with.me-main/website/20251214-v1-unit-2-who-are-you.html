1. Overview

A Note from the Founder (Jacksenn)

"After you've said hello and thank you, the next question people will ask is: 'Who are you?' And not in an existential way—they just want to know your name and where you're from. This is where real connection begins. When I told people I was from Germany, their eyes lit up. Suddenly, I wasn't just a tourist—I was a person with a story. This unit gives you the tools to tell that story, to say 'I am Alex from England,' and to ask others the same. It's simple, but it's powerful."

Learning Objectives

By the end of this unit, we will be able to:

  1. Introduce ourselves with our full name and origin.
  2. Ask someone their name and where they're from.
  3. Use the verb "biti" (to be) confidently in all six forms.
  4. Use the verb "zvati se" (to be called) confidently in all six forms.
  5. Name countries and nationalities in Serbian.
  6. Understand the basics of noun gender (masculine, feminine, neuter).
  7. Navigate real conversations about identity and origin.

3. Grammar (Gramatika)

A. Verb "biti" (to be) - Full Conjugation

The verb "biti" is the most important verb in Serbian. You learned three forms in Unit 1. Now we complete the picture.

Conjugation Table

Person Serbian English
Ja sam I am
Ti si You are (informal)
On/Ona/Ono je He/She/It is
Mi smo We are
Vi ste You are (formal/plural)
Oni/One/Ona su They are

Practical Examples

  • Ja sam iz Nemačke. (I am from Germany.)
  • Ti si turista. (You are a tourist.)
  • On je iz Srbije. (He is from Serbia.)
  • Mi smo studenti. (We are students.)
  • Vi ste iz Engleske? (Are you from England?)
  • Oni su iz Amerike. (They are from America.)

B. Verb "zvati se" (to be called) - Full Conjugation

The verb "zvati se" is a reflexive verb, which means it always comes with "se" (oneself). It's used to say your name.

Conjugation Table

Person Serbian English
Ja se zovem I am called
Ti se zoveš You are called (informal)
On/Ona/Ono se zove He/She/It is called
Mi se zovemo We are called
Vi se zovete You are called (formal/plural)
Oni/One/Ona se zovu They are called

Practical Examples

  • Ja se zovem Alex. (My name is Alex.)
  • Kako se zoveš? (What's your name? - informal)
  • Kako se zovete? (What's your name? - formal)
  • On se zove Marko. (His name is Marko.)
  • Ona se zove Ana. (Her name is Ana.)
  • Mi se zovemo Smith. (Our last name is Smith.)

C. Gender of Nouns - The Basics

In Serbian, every noun has a gender: masculine, feminine, or neuter. This affects adjectives and some verb forms.

How to Recognize Gender

Gender Typical Ending Examples
Masculine Consonant turista, student, Nemac, hotel
Feminine -a Nemačka, Srbija, studentkinja, žena
Neuter -o, -e ime, prezime, veče*

*Montenegro: veče (same)

Note: For now, we just need to recognize the pattern. We'll learn how gender affects grammar in later units.

Alex Dobar dan. Izvolite. Good day. Here you are.

Border Officer Prezime? Last name?

C. Cultural Note: Names and Introductions in Serbian Culture

  • Last names matter: In formal situations, people often use last names. "Gospodin Smith" (Mr. Smith) or "Gospođa Ana" (Mrs. Ana) are common.
  • "Drago mi je" is essential: This phrase ("Nice to meet you") is used in almost every introduction. Don't skip it!
  • Handshakes are standard: When meeting someone for the first time, a firm handshake and eye contact are expected.
  • Asking about origin is normal: Serbians and Montenegrins are genuinely curious about where you're from. It's not intrusive—it's friendly.
  • "Dobrodošao/Dobrodošla/Dobrodošli": Pay attention to gender! Use "Dobrodošao" for one man, "Dobrodošla" for one woman, and "Dobrodošli" for groups or formal situations.
