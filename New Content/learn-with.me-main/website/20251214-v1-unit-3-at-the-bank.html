1. Overview

A Note from the Founder (Jacksenn)

"The first time I had to change money, I felt like I was back in school, trying to remember numbers and currency names in a foreign language. It's stressful! But getting your local currency is a huge step towards feeling settled. This unit is our financial survival kit. We will learn the words and phrases to handle money confidently, so you can focus on enjoying your new life, not worrying about the exchange rate."

Learning Objectives

By the end of this unit, we will be able to:

  1. Use numbers from 11 to 100 to discuss amounts and prices.
  2. Ask for and understand currency exchange rates and amounts.
  3. Use the verb "trebati" (to need) to express necessities at the bank or post office.
  4. Identify and use essential financial vocabulary (bank, money, card, account).
  5. Navigate a simple transaction with a bank teller or at an ATM.
  6. Understand the difference between the Serbian Dinar and the Montenegrin Euro in conversation.

3. Grammar (Gramatika)

A. The Verb "trebati" (to need) - Our Necessity Tool

The verb "trebati" is crucial for expressing needs. It is often used impersonally, which is a key difference from English.

Conjugation Table: Trebati (to need)

The most common way we use this is the impersonal form: "Treba mi..." (It is needed to me... / I need...).

Serbian English Literal Translation Context
Treba mi pasoš. I need a passport. A passport is needed to me. Most common and simplest form.
Treba nam novac. We need money. Money is needed to us.
Treba ti kartica. You need a card. A card is needed to you.
Šta ti treba? What do you need? What is needed to you? Asking a friend.

Note: The noun that is needed (pasoš, novac, kartica) is in the Nominative case (the basic form), and the person who needs it (mi, nam, ti) is in the Dative case. We will focus on mastering the simple "Treba mi" for now.

B. Dialogue: Scheduling an Appointment at the Bank

Scene: Alex is at a bank in Belgrade, needing to open an account and schedule an appointment with a bank officer.

Role Serbian English
Alex Dobar dan. Želim da otvorim račun. Good day. I want to open an account.
Teller Dobar dan. Treba da zakažete sastanak. Good day. You need to schedule a meeting.
Alex U redu. Kada je slobodno? Alright. When is it free?
Teller Imamo slobodno u sredu u jedanaest sati. We have availability on Wednesday at eleven o'clock.
Alex Da li je slobodno u četvrtak? Is it free on Thursday?
Teller U četvrtak je slobodno u petnaest sati. On Thursday, it is free at fifteen o'clock (3 PM).
Alex U redu. Četvrtak u petnaest sati. Hvala! Alright. Thursday at fifteen o'clock. Thank you!
Teller Molim. Doviđenja. You're welcome. Goodbye.

C. Cultural Note: Currency and Banking

  • Currency Difference: Serbia uses the Dinar (RSD), while Montenegro uses the Euro (EUR). This is a crucial distinction for expats. We must be prepared to use the correct currency name and understand the different values.
  • Identification: For almost any transaction at a bank or exchange office, especially for foreigners, we will be asked for our Pasoš (passport). Always have it ready.
  • ATMs (Bankomat): ATMs are widely available, and using a card is often the simplest way to get local currency. However, knowing how to ask for the location of the Bankomat is essential.
