1. Overview

A Note from the Founder (Jacksenn)

"Being out and about in your new town for the first time—whether on vacation, a short stay, or after emigrating—is exciting. You have a few things in mind that you want to see, but you don't always know exactly where everything is. It's wonderful when you can ask someone, 'Where is the supermarket?' or 'Which way do I need to go?' and they answer, and you actually understand them. That's a great feeling, and it's the first step toward our next module."

Learning Objectives

By the end of this unit, we will be able to:

  1. Ask "Where is...?" for common places in our town or village.
  2. Use the verb "ići" (to go) confidently in all forms.
  3. Understand and give basic directions (left, right, straight ahead).
  4. Describe distances and locations using "near", "far", "here", and "there".
  5. Navigate a real conversation about finding places and getting around.
  6. Recognize the difference between urban and rural vocabulary in Serbia and Montenegro.

3. Grammar (Gramatika)

A. The Verb "IĆI" (To Go) - Our Most Important Movement Verb

The verb "ići" is essential for talking about movement and navigation. It's irregular, so we need to memorize its forms.

Conjugation Table

Person Serbian English
Ja idem I go
Ti ideš You go (informal)
On/Ona/Ono ide He/She/It goes
Mi idemo We go
Vi idete You go (formal/plural)
Oni/One/Ona idu They go

Practical Examples

  • Ja idem u centar. (I am going to the center.)
  • Ti ideš u školu? (Are you going to school?)
  • On ide na pijac. (He is going to the market.)
  • Mi idemo peške. (We are going on foot.) / Mi idemo pješke. (Montenegro)
  • Vi idete pravo. (You go straight.)
  • Oni idu u park. (They are going to the park.)

B. Asking "Where is...?" - The Essential Question

To ask where something is, we use the question word "gde" (or "gdje" in Montenegro) followed by the verb "je" (is).

The Basic Structure

Gde je + [place]? / Gdje je + [place]?

Examples

Serbian English
Gde* je pošta? Where is the post office?
Gde* je apoteka? Where is the pharmacy?
Gde* je park? Where is the park?
Gde* je autobuska stanica? Where is the bus station?

*Montenegro: Gdje

C. Giving Directions - Simple Commands

When giving directions, we use the imperative form of verbs. For now, we focus on the most common ones.

Imperative (You - informal) Imperative (You - formal/plural) English
Idi pravo. Idite pravo. Go straight.
Skreni levo*. Skrenite levo*. Turn left.
Skreni desno. Skrenite desno. Turn right.

*Montenegro: lijevo

Note: In this unit, we will primarily use the formal/plural form (idite, skrenite) as it's more polite when asking strangers for directions.

B. Dialogue 1: Finding the Pharmacy

Scene: Alex needs to find a pharmacy and asks a local woman for directions.

Role Serbian English
Alex Izvinite, gde je apoteka? Excuse me, where is the pharmacy?
Woman Apoteka? Idite pravo ovu ulicu, pa skrenite desno. The pharmacy? Go straight down this street, then turn right.
Alex Daleko je? Is it far?
Woman Ne, nije daleko. Pet minuta peške. No, it's not far. Five minutes on foot.
Alex Hvala vam puno! Thank you very much!
Woman Nema na čemu. Srećno! You're welcome. Good luck!

B. Dialogue 2: Lost in the Village

Scene: Alex is lost in a small village and asks an older man for help finding the bus station.

Role Serbian English
Alex Dobar dan. Ja sam izgubljen. Gde je autobuska stanica? Good day. I am lost. Where is the bus station?
Man Autobuska stanica je u centru. Vi ste ovde, na trgu. The bus station is in the center. You are here, at the square.
Alex Kako da dođem do centra? How can I get to the center?
Man Idite pravo, pa levo na uglu. Stanica je pored pošte. Go straight, then left at the corner. The station is next to the post office.
Alex Mogu li da idem peške? Can I walk there?
Man Da, možete. Deset minuta. Yes, you can. Ten minutes.
Alex Hvala vam mnogo! Thank you so much!
Man Nema problema. Doviđenja. No problem. Goodbye.

C. Cultural Note: Urban vs. Rural Life in Serbia and Montenegro

  • Grad vs. Selo: In Serbia and Montenegro, there is a strong cultural distinction between grad (town/city) and selo (village). Villages are often tight-knit communities where everyone knows each other, while cities are more anonymous and fast-paced.
  • Asking for Directions: Locals are generally very helpful when asked for directions. Don't be shy—most people will go out of their way to help you find what you're looking for.
  • "Peške" vs. "Pješke": In Montenegro, you'll hear "pješke" (on foot) instead of "peške". This is the Ijekavian variant.
  • The Importance of "Centar": The centar (center) is the heart of every town and village. Most important services (pošta, banka, apoteka) are located there.
  • "Trg" (Square): Many towns have a central square (trg) where people gather, socialize, and where local events take place. It's a great landmark for navigation.
