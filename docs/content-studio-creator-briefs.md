# Content Studio – Creator Briefs

Diese Datei sammelt die Creator-Brief-Prompts pro Unit. Jeder Block ist
copy-paste-fertig fuer die Maske unter `/admin/content-studio` (New Draft).

Konvention pro Unit:
- Module Number, Unit Number, Title, Unit description (max ~120 Zeichen)
- Creator brief / unit prompt in Code-Block, auf Englisch (Pipeline-Standard)

Bisheriger Stand: Modul 2 vollstaendig. Modul 3, 4, 5 werden auf Anfrage
nachgezogen.

## Modul 2 – Settling In

### Unit 2.1 – Apartment Viewing

- **Module Number:** 2
- **Unit Number:** 1
- **Title:** Apartment Viewing
- **Unit description:** View a small flat with the agent: rooms, balcony, first impression.

**Creator brief / unit prompt:**

```
- Situation: Three short scenes around an apartment viewing in Serbia or Montenegro: phone call to book the appointment with an agent, the actual walkthrough room by room, a soft polite first impression at the door.
- Prerequisites: greetings, biti/imati (Module 1 Units 1-2), Locative with u/na for places (Module 1 Units 5-6).
- New: possessive pronouns moj/tvoj/njegov/njen with full gender agreement; room and furniture vocabulary; phrases for soft first impressions (mi se svidja, bas svetlo, malo tijesno).
- Constraints: max 22 vocab items; exactly 3 dialogues; NO Genitive case yet (saved for next unit); NO restaurant context anywhere; vary first names across dialogues, do not repeat one name across all scenes.
- Montenegro footnotes: kirija (RS) vs. najam (ME); gdje vs. gde; ovdje vs. ovde.
- Founder note: short and honest about feeling lost at the first viewing, smiling and nodding without understanding.
```

### Unit 2.2 – Lease and Deposit

- **Module Number:** 2
- **Unit Number:** 2
- **Title:** Lease and Deposit
- **Unit description:** Sign the lease, negotiate the price, hand over the deposit without losing track.

**Creator brief / unit prompt:**

```
- Situation: Kitchen-table scene with the owner or agent. A paper contract, a calculator, an envelope with cash. Three scenes: stating the price, discussing deposit and notice period, signing with one last clarifying question.
- Prerequisites: possessive pronouns and room vocabulary (Unit 2.1).
- New: Genitive for quantities and prices (300 evra, sest meseci, dva kljuca); paukal rule (1 = nominative, 2-3-4 = singular Genitive, 5+ = plural Genitive); Genitive after bez / za / od / do / iz.
- Constraints: max 22 vocab; 3 dialogues; one clean comparison table for the paukal rule with five rows (1 / 2-4 / 5-20 / 21 / 22-24); do NOT introduce Dative; do NOT use any food or restaurant scenes.
- Montenegro footnotes: kirija/najam consistent with 2.1; popust same.
- Founder note: a short admission about getting numbers wrong and accidentally agreeing to twelve months instead of six.
```

### Unit 2.3 – Residence Permit

- **Module Number:** 2
- **Unit Number:** 3
- **Title:** Residence Permit
- **Unit description:** Submit papers at the foreigner office: forms, photos, the missing stamp.

**Creator brief / unit prompt:**

```
- Situation: The MUP in Serbia or the police office for foreigners in Montenegro. Three short scenes: handing over the folder, the clerk listing what is missing, scheduling the return.
- Prerequisites: Genitive for quantities and prices (Unit 2.2); formal Vi throughout.
- New: Genitive of negation with imati/biti (Imam pasos -> Nemam pasosa; Ima pecat -> Nema pecata); short bureaucratic imperatives (popunite, donesite, vratite se).
- Constraints: max 24 vocab; 3 dialogues; every dialogue stays strictly formal (Vi); show the imati / nemati and ima / nema pairs with at least 4 example pairs; the clerk is tired and brisk, never hostile.
- Montenegro footnotes: gdje/gde, ovdje/ovde, uput (RS) vs. uputnica (ME/BIH).
- Founder note: how "Nema pecata" felt like a personal insult, and how one grammar rule made every later visit less terrifying.
```

### Unit 2.4 – Opening a Bank Account

- **Module Number:** 2
- **Unit Number:** 4
- **Title:** Opening a Bank Account
- **Unit description:** Open a non-resident account: ID, PIN, IBAN readback, card pickup in five days.

**Creator brief / unit prompt:**

```
- Situation: A bank branch. Three scenes: choosing account type and fee plan, signing and setting the PIN with number-by-number readback, scheduling card pickup.
- Prerequisites: Genitive quantities and paukal (Unit 2.2); Genitive of negation (Unit 2.3).
- New: time and duration expressions with za + Accusative (za godinu dana, za sest meseci), na + Accusative (na sest meseci), kroz + Accusative (kroz pet radnih dana); reading and confirming numbers aloud.
- Constraints: max 24 vocab; 3 dialogues; one clean table contrasting za/na/kroz with examples; do NOT introduce conditionals or polite "I would like"; do NOT mix in restaurant or shopping scenes.
- Montenegro footnotes: filijala (RS) vs. ekspozitura (ME/BIH common).
- Founder note: the small humiliation of repeating the IBAN out loud while the banker corrects digit pronunciation.
```

### Unit 2.5 – Phone Contract and Internet

- **Module Number:** 2
- **Unit Number:** 5
- **Title:** Phone Contract and Internet
- **Unit description:** Pick a SIM and tariff, schedule the technician, survive small talk in the lift.

**Creator brief / unit prompt:**

```
- Situation: Two settings: a Telekom/MTEL/A1 shop and a technician installing internet at the new flat the next morning. Three scenes: comparing two tariffs out loud, scheduling the home visit, the technician at the door.
- Prerequisites: possessive pronouns (Unit 2.1); price/quantity Genitive (Unit 2.2).
- New: demonstratives ovaj / taj / onaj with full gender agreement; third-person possessives njegov / njen / njihov in Nominative.
- Constraints: max 22 vocab; 3 dialogues; two clean tables for demonstratives and third-person possessives; do NOT teach Dative; do NOT use restaurant scenes; sales tone is friendly-pushy, never adversarial.
- Montenegro footnotes: tarifa, instalacija, internet (all same), only annotate where actually different.
- Founder note: the strange intimacy of giving a stranger your WiFi password in a language you can barely speak.
```

### Unit 2.6 – Meeting the Neighbors

- **Module Number:** 2
- **Unit Number:** 6
- **Title:** Meeting the Neighbors
- **Unit description:** Stairwell handshake, lift small talk, accepting a parcel for the neighbor.

**Creator brief / unit prompt:**

```
- Situation: Three micro-encounters in the apartment building during the first week: stairwell handshake with an elderly neighbor, lift small talk with a same-age neighbor, doorbell where a neighbor asks the learner to accept a parcel.
- Prerequisites: greetings and biti (Module 1); possessive pronouns (Unit 2.1).
- New: Vocative case introduced fully for male and female names and for roles (Marko!, Ana, komsija, komsinice, gospodine, gospodjo); ritualised greeting exchange (Kako ste? - Dobro, hvala, a Vi?); switching between formal Vi and informal ti based on age cues.
- Constraints: max 26 vocab; 3 dialogues with clear formality contrast (Dialogue 1 formal, 2 informal, 3 mixed); two tables for Vocative (male / female); do NOT use Genitive grammar in any dialogue.
- Montenegro footnotes: komsija/komsinica, gospodin/gospodja, vikend all same; only annotate if actually different in dialogue lines.
- Founder note: the first time someone called the learner "komsija" and they realised they actually live here.
```

### Unit 2.7 – First Visit to the Doctor

- **Module Number:** 2
- **Unit Number:** 7
- **Title:** First Visit to the Doctor
- **Unit description:** Register at the local clinic, describe symptoms, get a referral.

**Creator brief / unit prompt:**

```
- Situation: The local primary-care clinic (Dom zdravlja). Three scenes: reception with a missing document, small talk in the waiting room with another patient, a five-minute consult.
- Prerequisites: Genitive of negation (Unit 2.3); formal Vi.
- New: Dative - restricted to two uses only: recipient (dajte mi, recite mi) and body sensations (hladno mi je, vruce mi je, boli me + Accusative); short Dative pronouns mi/ti/mu/joj/nam/vam/im.
- Constraints: max 26 vocab; 3 dialogues; one clean Dative-short-forms table; do NOT teach the imperative beyond "dajte mi / recite mi"; do NOT teach prescription grammar (conditionals, indirect speech); do NOT mix bank or MUP context into exercises.
- Montenegro footnotes: uput (RS) vs. uputnica (ME/BIH).
- Founder note: the terror of trying to describe "vrtoglavica" without knowing the word and the doctor patiently miming with their hands.
```

### Unit 2.8 – My First Month

- **Module Number:** 2
- **Unit Number:** 8
- **Title:** My First Month
- **Unit description:** Consolidation unit. No new grammar. Mix everything from Units 2.1–2.7.

**Creator brief / unit prompt:**

```
- Situation: The learner writes a short message to a friend back home about their first month, then has a coffee with a new neighbor who asks "Kako se snalazis?". Two dialogue scenes plus one written message of 6-8 sentences.
- Prerequisites: all four grammar anchors of Module 2 (possessives, Genitive quantity/negation, Vocative, Dative intro) and the vocabulary from Units 2.1-2.7.
- New: NOTHING. This unit only consolidates. Add a "Looking back" summary table at the top of the Grammar section listing the four Module 2 anchors with one canonical example each. Provide a small list of transition words the learner already knows (i, ali, jer, zato sto, pa, onda, takodje, na primer).
- Constraints: NO new vocabulary; NO Past tense (saved for Module 3); NO Future tense (saved for Module 4); each dialogue must visibly use at least 3 of the 4 Module 2 anchors.
- Montenegro footnotes: consistent with all previous units of this module, no new ones.
- Founder note: longer than usual (5-6 sentences) about the "one-month wall" - the moment most expats want to give up and why this is normal here. End with one concrete encouragement.
```
