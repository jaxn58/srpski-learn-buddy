## 3. Grammar

In this unit, we'll learn three fundamental grammar points that are essential for ordering food and drinks. We'll cover how to ask questions, how to correctly state what you want, and how to add modifications like "with milk" or "without sugar".

### A. Forming Questions with "li"

In Serbian, one of the most common ways to form a yes/no question is by using the question particle `li`. It's very simple: you place `li` directly after the verb.

Think of it as a flag that turns a statement into a question. The word order is typically: **Verb + li + ...?**

Let's look at the verb `imati` (to have). The form for the formal "you" (`vi`) is `imate`.

*   Statement: **Vi imate** kafu. (You have coffee.)
*   Question: **Imate li** kafu? (Do you have coffee?)

Here are a few more examples:
*   **Želite li** vodu? (Do you want water?)
*   **Donosite li** račun? (Are you bringing the bill?)
*   **Imate li** hladno mleko? (Do you have cold milk?)

### B. The Accusative Case for Ordering

When you order something, that "something" is the direct object of your sentence. In Serbian, direct objects take the **accusative case**. For now, we will focus on the most common change you'll see in a café.

Feminine nouns that end in `-a` in their basic form change their ending to `-u` in the accusative case.

*   Basic form: kafa (coffee), voda (water)
*   Accusative form: kaf**u**, vod**u**

So, when you say "I want a coffee," you must use the accusative form.

| Basic Form (Nominative) | Sentence using Accusative |
| :--- | :--- |
| Jedna **kafa** (One coffee) | Želim jednu **kafu**. (I want one coffee.) |
| Jedna **voda** (One water) | Molim jednu **vodu**. (One water, please.) |
| Jedna **pomorandža** (One orange) | Naručiću sok od **pomorandže**. (I will order orange juice.) |

Masculine and neuter nouns (like `espreso`, `kapućino`, `mleko`) often do not change their form in the singular accusative, which makes them easier to start with.

### C. Expressing "With" and "Without" (Instrumental & Genitive Cases)

To say you want something "with" or "without" an ingredient, you need to use prepositions that change the case of the noun that follows.

**1. "With" - `sa` + Instrumental Case**
The preposition **sa** means "with". It must be followed by a noun in the **instrumental case**. For masculine and neuter nouns, the ending usually changes to **-om**.

*   `mleko` (milk) becomes `sa mlekom` (with milk)
*   `šećer` (sugar) becomes `sa šećerom` (with sugar)

Examples:
*   Jednu kafu **sa mlekom**, molim. (One coffee with milk, please.)
*   Kapućino **sa šećerom**. (Cappuccino with sugar.)
*   Vodu **sa ledom**. (Water with ice.) - `led` (ice) becomes `ledom`.

**2. "Without" - `bez` + Genitive Case**
The preposition **bez** means "without". It must be followed by a noun in the **genitive case**. For many masculine nouns, the ending changes to **-a**.

*   `šećer` (sugar) becomes `bez šećera` (without sugar)
*   `mleko` (milk) becomes `bez mleka` (without milk)

Examples:
*   Espreso **bez šećera**, molim. (An espresso without sugar, please.)
*   Kafa **bez mleka**. (Coffee without milk.)
*   Voda **bez leda**. (Water without ice.) - `led` (ice) becomes `leda`.

## 6. Cultural Note: The Art of "Polako"

In Serbia and across the Balkans, having coffee is rarely a quick, rushed affair. It's a ritual. When you sit down at a `kafić`, you are expected to take your time. This is the essence of the word `polako`, which means "slowly" or "take it easy". Unlike in many Western cultures where coffee is often grabbed "to go," here it's about the experience: relaxing, watching the world go by, and most importantly, socializing. A single cup of coffee can be the reason for a meeting that lasts for hours. So, when you order your coffee, remember to relax and enjoy the moment. The waiter will not rush you to leave.
