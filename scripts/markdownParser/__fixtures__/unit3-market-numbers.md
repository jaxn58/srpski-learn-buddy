## 3. Grammar

This unit introduces two important concepts for shopping: asking for prices and expressing quantities.

### A. Asking for the Price: `koštati`

The verb **koštati** means "to cost". It's very simple to use.

For a singular item, you use the form **košta**.
*   **Koliko košta** ovaj sir? (How much does this cheese cost?)
*   **Koliko košta** hleb? (How much does bread cost?)
*   Izvinite, **koliko košta** maslinovo ulje? (Excuse me, how much does the olive oil cost?)

For plural items, you use the form **koštaju**.
*   **Koliko koštaju** masline? (How much do the olives cost?)
*   **Koliko koštaju** ove jabuke? (How much do these apples cost?)
*   Dobar dan, **koliko koštaju** domaći sirevi? (Good day, how much do the homemade cheeses cost?)

Notice the question word **koliko** (how much/how many) always comes first.

### B. The Genitive Case for Quantities

In English, we say "a kilogram *of* cheese". In Serbian, that word "of" is shown by changing the ending of the noun that follows the quantity. This new form is called the **Genitive Case**.

It sounds complex, but for shopping, you only need to learn a few simple patterns.

#### Masculine Nouns
**Rule:** For masculine nouns ending in a consonant, add **-a** to the end when they follow a quantity word like `kilogram`, `gram`, or `pola`.

| Base Form (Nominative) | Meaning | Quantity + Genitive | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| sir | cheese | kilogram **sira** | a kilogram of cheese |
| hleb | bread | pola **hleba** | half a loaf of bread |
| šećer | sugar | sto grama **šećera** | 100 grams of sugar |

**Examples in sentences:**
*   Želim jedan kilogram **sira**. (I want one kilogram of cheese.)
*   Treba mi sto grama **šećera**. (I need one hundred grams of sugar.)

#### A Quick Look at Feminine Nouns
You will also encounter feminine nouns, like `maslina` (olive). When you buy multiple olives, you use the plural form `masline`. However, after a quantity word, this also changes to a genitive form.

**Rule:** For `maslina`, the plural form `masline` (olives) becomes **maslina** after a quantity.

*   Molim vas, sto grama **maslina**. (One hundred grams of olives, please.)
*   Želim pola kilograma **maslina**. (I want half a kilogram of olives.)

For now, just remember these two patterns: add `-a` for masculine nouns like `sir`, and use `maslina` for "of olives".

### C. Forming Numbers 21-99

To form numbers from 21 to 99, you simply combine the word for the tens (`dvadeset`, `trideset`, etc.) with the word for the units (`jedan`, `dva`, `tri`, etc.), connecting them with **i** (and).

The structure is: **[Tens] i [Unit]**

*   21 = **dvadeset i jedan** (twenty and one)
*   35 = **trideset i pet** (thirty and five)
*   48 = **četrdeset i osam** (forty and eight)
*   99 = **devedeset i devet** (ninety and nine)

Note that the number **jedan** (one) changes for gender. If you are counting a feminine noun, you would use `jedna`, and for a neuter noun, `jedno`. For example: `dvadeset i jedna maslina` (21 olives).

## 4. Phrases (Practical Application)

Here are some practical phrases and dialogues you can use at the market.

### A. Essential Shopping Phrases

| Serbian | English |
| :--- | :--- |
| Mogu li da probam? | Can I try? |
| Koliko košta kilogram? | How much does a kilogram cost? |
| Želim pola kilograma. | I want half a kilogram. |
| Molim vas, dajte mi... | Please, give me... |
| Da li je ovo domaće? | Is this homemade? |
| Imate li svež hleb? | Do you have fresh bread? |
| To je sve, hvala. | That is all, thank you. |

### A. Dialogue 1: Asking to Try Cheese

| Role | Serbian | English |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **You** | Dobar dan. Mogu li da probam ovaj sir? | Good day. Can I try this cheese? |
| **Vendor** | Naravno, izvolite. | Of course, here you go. |
| **You** | Hvala. Odličan je. | Thank you. It's excellent. |
| **Vendor** | To je domaći, mladi sir. | That is homemade, young cheese. |

### B. Dialogue 2: Asking for the Price

| Role | Serbian | English |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **You** | Izvinite, koliko košta kilogram ovog sira? | Excuse me, how much does a kilogram of this cheese cost? |
| **Vendor** | Stari sir je deset evra. Mladi sir je osam evra. | The aged cheese is ten euros. The young cheese is eight euros. |
| **You** | U redu. A masline? Koliko koštaju? | Okay. And the olives? How much do they cost? |
| **Vendor** | Pet evra za kilogram. | Five euros for a kilogram. |

### C. Dialogue 3: Buying an Item

| Role | Serbian | English |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **You** | Dobro. Molim vas, dajte mi pola kilograma mladog sira. | Good. Please, give me half a kilogram of the young cheese. |
| **Vendor** | Izvolite. Još nešto? | Here you go. Anything else? |
| **You** | I sto grama maslina. To je sve. | And one hundred grams of olives. That is all. |
| **Vendor** | U redu. To je pet evra. | Okay. That is five euros. |

## 6. Cultural Note: The Pijaca

The `pijaca` (green market) is the heart and soul of Serbian daily life. It's more than just a place to buy food; it's a social hub where people meet, chat with vendors they've known for years, and get the freshest, seasonal produce directly from the farmers. Don't be afraid to ask "Da li je domaće?" (Is it homemade?). Vendors are usually very proud of their products and happy to offer you a taste. Shopping at the `pijaca` is a fantastic way to practice your Serbian and experience local culture firsthand.
